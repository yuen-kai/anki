1. BIOLOGY  — the study of living things
========================================================================

1.1 CELL BIOLOGY
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1.1.1 Cell Theory
  Description: The cell is the basic unit of life. Cell theory states
  that all living things are made of cells, cells are the smallest unit
  of life, and all cells come from pre-existing cells.

    Q B1.1-a) Which statement is part of cell theory?
       A) All cells contain chlorophyll
       B) All living things are made of cells
       C) Cells can appear from non-living matter
       D) Only animals are made of cells

    Q B1.1-b) The smallest unit that can be considered "alive" is the:
       A) atom      B) molecule      C) cell      D) organ

  1.1.2 Prokaryotes vs. Eukaryotes
  Description: Prokaryotic cells (e.g., bacteria) have no membrane-bound
  nucleus; their DNA floats freely. Eukaryotic cells (plants, animals,
  fungi) keep their DNA inside a nucleus and have membrane-bound
  organelles.

    Q B1.2-a) A key feature of a eukaryotic cell is:
       A) no DNA
       B) a membrane-bound nucleus
       C) it is always single-celled
       D) it has no organelles

    Q B1.2-b) Bacteria are best described as:
       A) eukaryotes      B) prokaryotes      C) viruses      D) fungi

    Q B1.2-c) Which organism is made of eukaryotic cells?
       A) A bacterium      B) A human      C) A virus      D) None

  1.1.3 Organelles
  Description: Organelles are specialized structures inside a cell. The
  mitochondria release energy from food, the nucleus stores DNA, and
  chloroplasts (in plants) carry out photosynthesis.

    Q B1.3-a) The organelle known as the cell's "powerhouse" is the:
       A) nucleus      B) ribosome      C) mitochondrion      D) vacuole

    Q B1.3-b) Chloroplasts are found mainly in:
       A) animal cells      B) plant cells      C) bacteria      D) viruses


1.2 GENETICS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1.2.1 DNA and Genes
  Description: DNA is the molecule that carries genetic instructions. A
  gene is a segment of DNA that codes for a trait. DNA is shaped like a
  double helix (a twisted ladder).

    Q B2.1-a) DNA is shaped like a:
       A) single line      B) double helix      C) sphere      D) triangle

    Q B2.1-b) A segment of DNA that codes for a trait is called a:
       A) cell      B) gene      C) protein      D) tissue

  1.2.2 Mendelian Inheritance
  Description: Traits pass from parents to offspring through alleles.
  Dominant alleles mask recessive ones. An organism with two different
  alleles is heterozygous; two identical alleles make it homozygous.

    Q B2.2-a) A dominant allele:
       A) is always harmful
       B) masks a recessive allele
       C) only appears in plants
       D) cannot be inherited

    Q B2.2-b) An organism with two identical alleles for a trait is:
       A) heterozygous      B) homozygous      C) recessive      D) mutated


1.3 EVOLUTION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1.3.1 Natural Selection
  Description: Individuals with traits better suited to their environment
  tend to survive and reproduce more. Over many generations, helpful
  traits become more common in a population.

    Q B3.1-a) Natural selection favors individuals that are:
       A) the largest
       B) best suited to their environment
       C) born first
       D) fully identical to each other

    Q B3.1-b) Natural selection acts over:
       A) a single day
       B) many generations
       C) one lifetime only
       D) it does not involve time


1.4 ECOLOGY
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1.4.1 Food Chains and Energy Flow
  Description: Energy flows from producers (plants) to consumers.
  Herbivores eat plants; carnivores eat animals. Energy is lost as heat
  at each step, so food chains are usually short.

    Q B4.1-a) In a food chain, plants are usually the:
       A) top predators      B) producers      C) decomposers      D) consumers

    Q B4.1-b) An animal that eats only plants is a:
       A) carnivore      B) herbivore      C) producer      D) decomposer


========================================================================
2. CHEMISTRY  — the study of matter and its changes
========================================================================

2.1 MATTER
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  2.1.1 States of Matter
  Description: Matter commonly exists as solid, liquid, or gas. Solids
  hold their shape, liquids take the shape of their container, and gases
  spread to fill any space. Adding or removing heat changes the state.

    Q C1.1-a) Which state of matter has a fixed shape and fixed volume?
       A) gas      B) liquid      C) solid      D) plasma

    Q C1.1-b) Heating a liquid enough turns it into a:
       A) solid      B) gas      C) mixture      D) metal

  2.1.2 Elements, Compounds, and Mixtures
  Description: An element is a pure substance made of one type of atom.
  A compound is two or more elements chemically bonded (e.g., water).
  A mixture is substances physically combined and separable (e.g., salad).

    Q C1.2-a) Water (H2O) is an example of a:
       A) element      B) compound      C) mixture      D) metal

    Q C1.2-b) Which is a pure substance made of only one kind of atom?
       A) compound      B) mixture      C) element      D) solution


2.2 ATOMIC STRUCTURE
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  2.2.1 Parts of the Atom
  Description: Atoms contain protons (positive), neutrons (neutral) in
  the nucleus, and electrons (negative) orbiting outside. The number of
  protons defines the element.

    Q C2.1-a) The particle with a positive charge is the:
       A) electron      B) neutron      C) proton      D) photon

    Q C2.1-b) Electrons carry a charge that is:
       A) positive      B) negative      C) neutral      D) variable

    Q C2.1-c) What determines which element an atom is?
       A) number of neutrons
       B) number of protons
       C) number of electrons only
       D) its temperature

  2.2.2 The Periodic Table
  Description: The periodic table organizes elements by atomic number
  (number of protons). Rows are periods; columns are groups. Elements in
  the same group often behave similarly.

    Q C2.2-a) Elements in the periodic table are ordered by:
       A) weight in grams
       B) atomic number
       C) color
       D) alphabetical name

    Q C2.2-b) A vertical column in the periodic table is called a:
       A) period      B) group      C) row      D) bond


2.3 CHEMICAL BONDING
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  2.3.1 Ionic and Covalent Bonds
  Description: In an ionic bond, one atom transfers electrons to another
  (common between metals and non-metals). In a covalent bond, atoms share
  electrons (common between non-metals).

    Q C3.1-a) In a covalent bond, atoms:
       A) transfer electrons      B) share electrons
       C) lose their nucleus      D) never interact

    Q C3.1-b) An ionic bond usually forms between a:
       A) metal and a non-metal
       B) two identical gases
       C) two liquids only
       D) two mixtures


2.4 REACTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  2.4.1 Acids and Bases
  Description: Acids have a pH below 7 and taste sour (e.g., lemon juice);
  bases have a pH above 7 and feel slippery (e.g., soap). A pH of 7 is
  neutral, like pure water.

    Q C4.1-a) A substance with a pH of 3 is:
       A) a base      B) neutral      C) an acid      D) a metal

    Q C4.1-b) Pure water has a pH of about:
       A) 0      B) 7      C) 14      D) 4

    Q C4.1-c) A pH above 7 indicates a:
       A) strong acid      B) base      C) neutral solution      D) gas

3. PHYSICS  — the study of matter, energy, and motion
========================================================================

3.1 MOTION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  3.1.1 Speed, Velocity, and Acceleration
  Description: Speed is how fast something moves (distance / time).
  Velocity is speed with a direction. Acceleration is a change in
  velocity over time (speeding up, slowing down, or turning).

    Q P1.1-a) Speed is calculated as:
       A) time / distance
       B) distance / time
       C) mass x speed
       D) force x time

    Q P1.1-b) Velocity differs from speed because it includes:
       A) mass      B) direction      C) color      D) temperature

  3.1.2 Newton's Laws of Motion
  Description: First law: objects keep doing what they're doing unless a
  force acts (inertia). Second law: force = mass x acceleration. Third
  law: every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

    Q P1.2-a) Newton's third law states that every action has:
       A) no reaction
       B) an equal and opposite reaction
       C) a stronger reaction
       D) a delayed reaction

    Q P1.2-b) The equation F = m x a comes from Newton's:
       A) first law      B) second law      C) third law      D) law of gravity

    Q P1.2-c) The tendency of an object to resist changes in motion is:
       A) friction      B) gravity      C) inertia      D) energy


3.2 ENERGY
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  3.2.1 Kinetic and Potential Energy
  Description: Kinetic energy is the energy of motion. Potential energy
  is stored energy due to position (e.g., a ball held up high). Energy
  can change from one form to another.

    Q P2.1-a) A moving car has mostly:
       A) potential energy      B) kinetic energy
       C) no energy             D) chemical energy only

    Q P2.1-b) A rock resting at the top of a hill has stored:
       A) kinetic energy      B) potential energy
       C) sound energy        D) no energy

  3.2.2 Conservation of Energy
  Description: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed
  from one type to another. The total amount of energy stays constant.

    Q P2.2-a) The law of conservation of energy says energy can be:
       A) created from nothing
       B) destroyed completely
       C) transformed but not destroyed
       D) only stored, never moved


3.3 FORCES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  3.3.1 Gravity and Friction
  Description: Gravity is a force that pulls objects toward each other;
  on Earth it pulls things toward the ground. Friction is a force that
  opposes motion between surfaces in contact.

    Q P3.1-a) The force that pulls a dropped object toward the ground is:
       A) friction      B) gravity      C) inertia      D) magnetism

    Q P3.1-b) Friction is a force that:
       A) speeds objects up
       B) opposes motion between surfaces
       C) only acts in space
       D) has no effect on movement


3.4 WAVES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  3.4.1 Sound and Light
  Description: Waves carry energy from place to place. Sound waves need a
  medium (like air) to travel; light waves can travel through empty space.
  Light travels much faster than sound.

    Q P4.1-a) Sound waves need a medium such as air because they:
       A) are made of light
       B) cannot travel through a vacuum
       C) travel faster than light
       D) have no energy

    Q P4.1-b) Compared to sound, light travels:
       A) slower      B) at the same speed      C) much faster      D) not at all
